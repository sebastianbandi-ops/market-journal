Which words did the trading system actually hear?

By Sebastian Bandi · November 18, 2026

A trading system should show which terms the traders agreed to and which ones it inferred. That’s the change I’d make to the trade ticket described in Opportune’s article.

The danger of a familiar answer

Opportune describes software that can extract terms from a conversation and suggest missing fields using a trader’s history. The trader reviews the proposed entry before it becomes the official record.

I can see the appeal on a busy desk. Entering a delivery location once saves time for the trader, and a clean record gives scheduling and accounting teams the same instructions.

Imagine a trader who usually buys gas at one hub but switches locations today. If the message omits the hub, the system could book the usual location with nobody noticing.

Give the reviewer a better question

A screen full of completed fields invites a quick glance and a click. I’d want each suggested term to show whether it came from today’s conversation or from a pattern in older deals.

For the delivery hub, I’d give the reviewer the relevant line of the conversation. If neither party named a hub, the next step would be to confirm it with the counterparty.

To judge a pilot, I’d track minutes saved by traders and corrections made by schedulers. A desk gains little if someone downstream spends the afternoon chasing a missing delivery term.
