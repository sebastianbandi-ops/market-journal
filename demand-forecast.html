Who pays for a faster grid connection?

By Sebastian Bandi · November 18, 2026

A data center developer wants a date when it can turn on the servers. The people already paying electric bills in that area have a different question. Who pays for the upgrades if the developer walks away?

What an earlier estimate buys

On November 16, PJM proposed studying new generators and large electricity customers together. The regional grid operator says it could shorten a two-year study to one year and identify upgrade costs earlier.

Read the original article · PJM Inside Lines, November 16, 2026

I think that second change deserves more attention. An early estimate gives a town weighing a data center proposal something concrete to discuss while it still has room to negotiate.

Suppose a utility needs a larger substation to serve a proposed campus. If the customer commits to only one of three buildings, the utility still needs to decide how much equipment to order.

The question at the public hearing

I would ask to see two bills for that substation. One assumes all three buildings open on schedule. The other assumes only the first opens. Whose monthly payment changes between those cases?

PJM also proposes requiring financial commitments earlier. That could discourage speculative applications, although a smaller developer with a credible project might have a harder time putting up the money early.

If regulators approve the proposal, I’d judge it by what a community knows before it commits. A shorter study is valuable. An estimate that identifies who pays if plans change is worth more.
