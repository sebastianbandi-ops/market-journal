A transformer belongs in the financing meeting

By Sebastian Bandi · November 18, 2026

When should a utility order a transformer for a line it still needs permission to build? Order early and customers could pay for idle equipment. Wait for approval and the factory slot may disappear.

The cost of waiting in line

The International Energy Agency surveyed the transmission supply chain and found that procurement times for cables and large transformers had nearly doubled since 2021. Some specialized direct-current cables took more than five years to obtain.

Read the original article · International Energy Agency, February 25, 2025

I read that finding as a financing problem. A lender can model the cost of an expensive transformer fairly easily. A delivery date that keeps moving changes when the entire project can start earning revenue.

Consider a wind farm that finishes construction while its grid connection is delayed. The turbines may be ready, but the developer still owes interest while it waits to sell its first electricity.

Who can use the order if plans change?

One option I’d explore is whether several planned substations can use the same transformer specification. If permits delay one site, another might take the earlier delivery and put it to work.

That idea has limits. Voltage requirements, transport routes and foundation designs can differ. I would want engineers to identify a realistic second destination before anyone counted on the flexibility in a financing decision.

At the next project review, I’d put the manufacturer’s delivery window beside the loan repayment schedule. Seeing both dates together makes a useful question unavoidable. Who can afford six months of delay?
