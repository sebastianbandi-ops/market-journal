What an auction chart leaves out

By Sebastian Bandi

The tallest bar in my auction chart belongs to 2025. It is tempting to call that the most profitable year on the screen. The chart gives me no basis for that claim.

Start with the two locations

PJM’s introduction to financial transmission rights explains why a power buyer might want one. A transmission constraint can create a difference in the congestion portion of electricity prices between two locations. Read PJM’s introduction to financial transmission rights.

A financial transmission right gives its holder a financial position tied to that difference in the day-ahead market. To understand the position, I need to know where it starts, where it ends and which hours it covers. PJM FTR Center.

Give the chart a narrower job

I built the annual view to compare cleared activity. The bars show buy and sell quantities in megawatts, while the line counts cleared records. None of those measures tells me what a participant earned.

There is a second trap at the right edge. The 2026 column includes monthly files only through August. For a fair comparison I would pull the same months from earlier years.

If I were reviewing a particular hedge, I would start with the delivery terms, match the path and hours, then compare its auction cost with the congestion payments over the holding period. The annual chart helps me decide where to look next.
