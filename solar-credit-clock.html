What is one month worth on a solar project?

By Sebastian Bandi · November 18, 2026

A month of delay can cost a solar developer a month of electricity sales. SEIA’s explanation of the federal tax changes made me ask what else a developer could lose by missing the deadline.

Which solar projects face the December 2027 deadline?

SEIA describes two ways a solar project can meet the timing requirement for federal clean electricity credits. A project that missed the July 2026 construction cutoff has until the end of 2027 to enter service.

Imagine two otherwise identical solar projects that both missed the first cutoff. One expects to start selling electricity in September 2027. The other expects to start in December. Which one would a lender prefer?

Both developers could show the same construction budget and projected annual generation. The September project also has three months to absorb a late delivery or a failed inspection. That cushion has financial value.

Who controls the last month?

SEIA writes from the perspective of the solar industry and argues that the changes will slow deployment. I think the deadlines could also change which projects get built first.

A lender might favor a site with permits in hand over a sunnier site awaiting an interconnection upgrade. The difference could come down to whether anybody can credibly commit to an energization date.

Before choosing a bid, I’d ask each developer to model its first delivery in January 2028. Seeing what that delay costs would help me weigh a promise to finish on time.
